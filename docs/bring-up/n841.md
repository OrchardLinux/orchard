# iPhone XR (N841, A12) bring-up

What it took to get Orchard onto the iPhone XR, the first A12 board, and what
is still missing. The other nine A12 boards share the chip-level parts
(`t8020.dtsi`, the boot route, m1n1) and have only been built, not run.

Sources: [Project Cascadia](https://github.com/rzxvx/Project-Cascadia)
(`devices/a12`, dr1408's iPhone XR port and rzxvx's base) and
[badapple](https://forgejo.toe.quest/kiki/badapple) (kieroid's iPhone XS Max
port, where most A12 kernel work comes from). What was taken from where is in
each kernel and m1n1 commit.

## State

| Part | State | Notes |

| Boot from DFU | works | usbliter8 board, `ibss` route, about 12 s to Linux |
| Install, launch | works | `orchard install --kernel --m1n1 --data`, `orchard launch --m1n1` |
| CPUs, frequency scaling | works | six cores; thermal zones throttle from 85 °C |

| Backlight | works | DWI, as on the iPhone 8; a backlight device with 2047 levels |

## Getting in

The A12's boot ROM is not open to checkm8. A board running
usbliter8 (an RP2350; this was a
B4 Pico Ultra) enters it with the phone plugged into the board, and passes the
phone's USB and serial console through to the computer.

- From a running iOS, one press of the board's STAR button puts the phone into
  entered DFU; `orchard devices` then shows `usbliter8` under Pwned.
- It does not work while iOS is jailbroken with Dopamine. Restart iOS first.
- One replug between runs helps. A long press of STAR leaves DFU.
- The board's own serial port (`/dev/cu.usbmodem*`, 115200) carries the phone's
  UART. Record it during every boot.

From there the `ibss` route goes on as on the other chips: Apple's iBSS,
patched, then m1n1 as iBoot's restore kernel. Two things differ on the A12:

- The iBSS is patched by Cascadia's "anchor" patcher, with the manifest check
  opened (`any_manifest`).
- No ticket for the A12 is needed: the A11's (0x8015) is accepted.

The six A12 iPads' iPadOS 18.6.2 iBSS is not handled by that patcher yet.

## Commands

Until the kernel, m1n1 and board data are merged and published, each is named
by hand:

```sh
orchard --data ORCHARD_CHECKOUT install omarchy-a \
    --kernel LINUX_TREE --device n841 --m1n1 m1n1-idevice.macho --ibss
orchard --data ORCHARD_CHECKOUT launch --ibss --m1n1 m1n1-idevice.macho
```

Build m1n1 as `boot/m1n1/build` does, with `EXTRA_CFLAGS=-DQUIET_BOOT`; without
it m1n1 prints its log over the splash.

To try a kernel on the installed system in one DFU round, boot it from RAM
with the installed root:

```sh
orchard run --device n841 --ibss --m1n1 m1n1-idevice.macho \
    --kernel Image.gz --dtb t8020-n841.dtb --initramfs INITRD \
    --bootargs "root=PARTUUID=… rw rootwait fbcon=map:9 vt.global_cursor_default=0"
```

A changed module needs no DFU at all: push the `.ko` and reload it.

## What each part needed

### Storage

The A12's storage controller sits between the A11's and the M1's: the M1's
NVMMU, 64 translation blocks deep, in front of the A11's ring queues of 16
entries. Beyond that:

- The SART (version 1) has SART2's registers; the queues and translation
  blocks need SART entries of their own.
- The admin queue is programmed before the coprocessor starts, because the
  firmware fetches from the queue left in the registers at once.
- The queue count is asked for before the IO queues are created, and each IO
  queue's base is also written to its register.
- Power: `ps_apcie_st` needs `ps_apcie_gp` and `ps_pcie_ref` as parents.

m1n1 needed the same three parts (coprocessor wrapper `iop,ascwrap-v2`, SART
version 1, the NVMe handling) to read the boot partition.

### Display

The boot loader's framebuffer is XRGB2101010, but sRGB values cannot be copied
into it. Its channels are in Apple's extended range (384 black, 895 white) and
the display pipe takes them as linear light. iOS's driver says so itself:

```sh
idevicediagnostics ioregentry AppleCLCD     # from a running iOS, no jailbreak
```

reports `enableLinearToPanel` and a separate degamma stage, which the boot
loader leaves in passthrough. simpledrm converts for a framebuffer marked
`apple,extended-range-linear`.

- Stock conversion (sRGB at full range): black up to 37 %, white from 87 %.
- sRGB squeezed into the range without linearising: every midtone too bright.

Both were tried first, by eye, and both were wrong. Read the driver's registry
before experimenting.

Not done: the panel is a P3 one and sRGB's primaries are not mapped into it.
The iPhone XS Max has the same plane format word (0x03dc8000) and very likely
needs the same marking.

### Touch

A HID-over-SPI controller behind an HBPP boot loader.

- The boot loader speaks SPI mode 3; the firmware mode 0.
- The firmware's last (start) write is acknowledged with any value.
- The interrupt line never signals, so the driver polls (8 ms idle, 4 ms after
  a report) and drops repeated packets.
- `ps_sio` and `ps_sio_busif` must stay out of automatic power gating, or SPI1
  does not come up again.

### Wi-Fi and Bluetooth

A BCM4357 (PCI 14e4:440a) with Bluetooth on UART1.

- PCIe devices are powered before the ports train their links.
- The chip's OTP sits in the GCI core, Apple's record at 0x1110. From it the
  driver learns the module maker (Murata or USI) and asks for that maker's
  NVRAM; Bluetooth takes the maker from the Wi-Fi node for its patch file.
- The Bluetooth firmware lists LE extended scanning and then rejects it
  ("Opcode 0x2041 failed"); the chip has its own compatible,
  `brcm,bcm4357-bt`, for that. Its address comes from the loader, through the
  `bluetooth0` alias.

### Backlight

Two LM3539 LED drivers light the panel and take their level from DWI
(`/arm-io/dwi`), as on the iPhone 8. The loader leaves them enabled at level
1429 of 2047.

### Sensors and power

- Nine die temperature sensors in three blocks; the offsets are in the Apple
  device tree. One of the power manager's reads nothing and is hidden.
- The SMC gives battery, charger, the power and volume buttons and restart.
  Its regulator keys take the function's code with the state in bit 0.

## Open

- **Wi-Fi over sleep**: the firmware never acknowledges the host's D3
  notice. Its firmware reports shared area version 7 with flags 0x70010007.
  Neither way of sending the notice gets an answer: the mailbox word in the
  shared area (the device-to-host word stays 0 when polled), nor a control
  message of type 0x23, which is where firmware without the mailbox flag
  (0x2000000) is said to take it. Until the handshake iOS uses is known,
  brcmfmac resets the chip for sleep and loads its firmware again on wake
  (under a second; the network reconnects by itself), so Wi-Fi is not
  reachable while the phone sleeps.
- **Resume**: touch and Wi-Fi load their firmware again after wake in the
  background (touch 0.5 s, Wi-Fi under a second) so resume itself takes
  under 0.1 s. Touch's first power-up after its supplies were off answers
  3e 02 instead of the bootloader whatever the delays; the second works.
- **Power button**: macsmc-input now reports the press that woke the
  system, as gpio-keys does; before, a second press was needed to light
  the screen.
- **Power in sleep**: idle cores power down with state retention (CYC_OVRD
  WFI mode 3, as iOS's idle), and the panel is switched off over sleep the
  way iOS does it (DCS display off/sleep in, timing generator stopped, panel
  reset and its Chestnut rail off; on wake rail, reset, sleep out in command
  mode, high-speed clock, timing generator, 100 ms, display on, video mode;
  no vendor commands). Unplugged sleep draw: 148 mA before the panel work,
  120 mA after. The power management coprocessor (PMP) does not run under
  Linux (its ASC CPU_CONTROL reads 0; the SMC's reads 0x10); its firmware
  is the `fwFirmwareImage` section of the kernelcache's
  `com.apple.driver.AppleT8020PMPFirmware`, a raw RTKit image run from IOP
  address 0. On the iPhone X booting the PMP alone saved about half a watt.
  Reading the PMP's region (0x23b500000) from Linux while it is off took the
  NVMe controller down.
- **System level cache**: iBoot leaves the memory controller's cache off;
  Linux turns it on as iOS does (broadcast, 16 ways, poll each plane) and
  off before a restart. Dark idle about 176 mA with it, 183 mA without.
- **Suspend to RAM**: Linux can take cores offline and start them again
  through m1n1 (PMGR stop/start, non-posted writes; m1n1 cleans its state to
  RAM at handoff and keeps a resume slot). S2R entry reaches the last WFI,
  but the SoC never sleeps for real: on our DFU route the SEP stays powered
  (PMGR ps 0x80600 actual 0xf) and refuses a power-off from the AP, and iOS
  only enters S2R after SEPOS powered the SEP off. The AP wake mask (PMGR
  reg[2] +0x8..+0x10) was already open.
- **Touch interrupt**: the line works once every packet read is answered
  with a bare status word in the same chip-select window (iOS's
  AppleHIDTransportSPI), and once the AOP GPIO block routes it to group 6
  (the AP may use 4-6 only). Idle touch costs nothing now; dark idle 169 mA.
- **m1n1 with every board's device tree**: an asynchronous SError while it
  checks the 22nd tree of a full payload, seen on the iPhone XR only. A
  payload with one tree boots.
- **Chainloading m1n1** through a running m1n1 hangs; an m1n1 change needs a
  DFU round.
- **A13**: entered by the same board, but its iBSS is encrypted and no key is
  in the board data.
